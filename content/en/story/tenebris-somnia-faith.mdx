## Gameplay: Retro Pixels Meet Live-Action Nightmares

Tenebris Somnia's calling card is the collision of two formats. Part of the time you are navigating a 2D pixel-art world, hunting for items and solving the puzzles that gate your progress. Then the game cuts to live-action footage shot by a professional film crew, and the tonal whiplash is entirely the point.

| Pillar | What to expect |
|---|---|
| Exploration | 2D environments to comb for clues, keys, and tools |
| Puzzles | Logic and item puzzles that gate progression |
| Combat | Fights against horrible creatures, likely with scarce resources |
| Story | A disturbing narrative revealed slowly rather than explained upfront |
| Cutscenes | Live-action sequences shot by an award-winning crew |
| Tone | Mature horror; player discretion advised |

The live-action element is why the game draws so much attention — and, occasionally, so much suspicion. Shooting real footage for an indie horror game is expensive and slow, and doing it with a decorated crew raises the production floor considerably. It also produces the uncanny effect the game is chasing: real faces and real rooms sitting beside sprite work, refusing to blend cleanly. The Tenebris Somnia faith crossover does not change any of those mechanics, but it does explain why the tone feels familiar to a certain kind of horror fan.
