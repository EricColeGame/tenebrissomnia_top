Before you can theorize about how a story ends, you need to understand what kind of story it is. Tenebris Somnia is a retro-styled survival horror game built by a six-person team at Saibot Studios, working alongside collaborators from the film industry. Project director Tobías Rusjan has described programming the game in Unreal Engine using Blueprints, while co-creator Andrés Borghi rounds out the core creative partnership.

## How Live-Action Cutscenes Could Shape the Tenebris Somnia Ending

The single most distinctive thing about this game is its use of live-action cutscenes inside a low-poly, retro-styled horror experience. That stylistic clash is not just a gimmick — it is a storytelling tool, and it has real implications for how the ending might work.

Consider what live-action footage does to a player's sense of reality. When the game shifts from blocky polygons to a real human face, the player is pulled out of the game world and into something that feels more immediate. Classic horror games have used similar tricks with pre-rendered video, but shooting with actual actors and real sets creates a different, rawer texture.

| Technique | Effect on the Player | Why It Matters for the Finale |
|---|---|---|
| Sudden shift to live action | Disorientation, unease | Makes a late twist harder to predict |
| Real actors vs. low-poly models | Emotional contrast | Humanizes a character before a reveal |
| Film grain and lighting | Illusion of "found" footage | Suggests an unreliable narrator |
| Repeated cutscene imagery | Pattern recognition | Plants clues across the whole game |

If the developers are building toward a reveal, the live-action format gives them a tool most indie horror games simply do not have. It also raises the bar: a weak ending would feel especially jarring after such a distinctive setup.
