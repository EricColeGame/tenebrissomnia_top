Tenebris Somnia is described as a puzzle-driven adventure, which means progress is gated by logic rather than reflexes. Puzzle difficulty is the biggest lever on total playtime. A player who solves everything independently can easily double the time of someone following a walkthrough, because stuck time compounds — you lose twenty minutes on one puzzle, then another thirty later.

### Live-Action Cutscenes and FMV Pacing

The game blends 2D gameplay with live-action cutscenes. FMV sequences are fixed-length, so they add a predictable baseline to every playthrough. ### Combat, Deaths, and Resource Scarcity

## Practical Tips for Beating Tenebris Somnia Efficiently

Whether you want a tight six-hour run or a full completionist sweep, a few habits will save you real time.

- **Read everything.** Notes and documents in horror games are usually puzzle keys in disguise. Skipping them creates backtracking later.
- **Sweep rooms before advancing.** A two-minute check for supplies now can prevent a twenty-minute death spiral later.
- **Bank healing items.** Use consumables only when a fight is unavoidable. Survival horror punishes panic-healing.
- **Keep a manual puzzle log.** Writing down codes, symbols, and combinations you find is faster than re-walking a zone to re-read them.
- **Watch cutscenes on run one.** FMV scenes often carry context that makes later puzzles obvious.
- **Save before unknown rooms.** Quick saves are your cheapest insurance against a wasted hour.

| Phase of Play | Recommended Approach | Time Saved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First hour | Explore fully, hoard supplies | 30–60 min later |
| Mid-game puzzles | Log every code and symbol | 30–90 min |
| Combat zones | Retreat when resources are low | 20–60 min |
| Pre-finale | Clear optional rooms before the point of no return | 1–2 hrs on a completionist run |
| Second playthrough | Skip cutscenes, use known routes | 40–50% faster |
