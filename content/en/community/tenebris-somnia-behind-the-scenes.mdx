## Inside the Pipeline: Practical First, Post-Effects Second

Based on the developer's description, the production follows a hybrid model: build the effect for real, then enhance it digitally. This is general production logic applied to what the team described — they explicitly noted both practical FX/SFX work and post-effects.

| Stage | What typically happens | Why horror benefits |
|---|---|---|
| Concept and planning | Shot lists, creature design, lighting plans | Prevents expensive on-set guesswork |
| On-set practical FX | Physical makeup, prosthetics, wardrobe, set dressing | Gives actors something real to react to |
| Cinematography | Framing, darkness, controlled light | Hides seams and builds tension |
| Post-effects | Cleanup, compositing, color, subtle digital augmentation | Fixes imperfections without replacing the effect |
| Sound and mix | Layered audio, stingers, silence | Often carries more fear than the visual |

The important pattern here is that post-production is used as a *polish*, not a *replacement*. When a creature is physically present on set, the lighting behaves correctly, shadows fall naturally, and the actor's performance is grounded in something tangible. Digital cleanup then removes the rough edges — a wire, a seam, a distracting highlight — without erasing the physicality that made the shot work.

### The Order of Operations Matters

A common mistake in low-budget horror is shooting flat and "fixing it later." The Tenebris Somnia approach appears to invert that: commit to the effect on set, then refine. That sequencing is why the results read as cinematic rather than synthetic.

## Practical Effects vs. Digital Effects in Horror

Players often frame this as a binary: practical or CGI. In practice, most effective horror uses both. Here is how the two approaches compare on the dimensions that actually matter during production.

| Dimension | Practical effects | Digital effects | Hybrid approach |
|---|---|---|---|
| Realism of light and shadow | Very high | Depends on artist skill | High |
| Actor performance | Strong — something is there | Weaker without reference | Strong |
| Cost profile | Front-loaded (crew, materials) | Back-loaded (artist hours) | Balanced |
| Flexibility after shooting | Low | Very high | Moderate to high |
| Risk on set | Higher — fewer retakes possible | Lower | Moderate |
| Best use | Creature presence, wardrobe, makeup | Cleanup, augmentation, impossible shots | Most horror cinematics |

The hybrid column is where Tenebris Somnia sits, according to the developer's own description. That is also the industry-standard answer for creature work: shoot it real, then fix it in post.
